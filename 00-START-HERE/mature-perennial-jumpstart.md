# 00-START-HERE / 03-FOOD-FOREST — The Mature-Perennial Jumpstart
*Skip the wait. Bring mature perennials to year one: buy/transplant/divide
already-maturing plants so fruit development starts immediately — and keep
every community's nursery perpetually producing transplants for the next one.*

## The Principle

The classic food-forest timeline problem: fruit trees take 3–7 years,
berries 1–3, asparagus 3 — "year one" is usually an exercise in patience.
The jumpstart strategy flips it: **plant age, not seeds.** A mature perennial
transplanted correctly fruits the same year or next — year one becomes a
harvest year.

The network compounds this: **every community is both a farm and a nursery**,
and mature divisions/transplants are part of what travels the surplus↔need
routes. The mature-plant stream is a standing commodity of the network.

## The Year-One Fruit Techniques (per method)

### 1. Buy & transplant mature stock
- **Bare-root trees 3–5 years old** (not 1-year whips): fruit 1–2 years
  earlier; bare-root season (winter/dormant) keeps cost and shock low
- **Container trees/brambles already fruiting** — a potted mature blueberry
  or fig transplants with fruit set intact; you literally move the harvest
- **Where to get them cheap:** end-of-season nursery sales (50–80% off),
  landscaping contractor culls, orchard removals/resales, and — the network
  way — other communities' nursery surpluses

### 2. Divide & transplant mature plants (nearly free)
Many perennials *are* mature the moment you divide them:
- **Berries:** strawberry runners from any mature bed fruit NEXT spring;
  blackberry/raspberry root cuttings fruit year 2 at most
- **Herbaceous medicinals & food:** comfrey, mint, rhubarb, horseradish,
  asparagus crowns (1–2 yr crowns cut the 3-year wait), artichokes —
  division = instant mature plant
- **Aromatic guild plants:** rosemary, lavender, sage cuttings from mature
  plants hit production in 6–12 months

### 3. Grafting — the master key (year-one fruit from year-one wood)
- **Graft mature/fruiting-variety scionwood onto young rootstock** (or onto
  existing seedlings on the land): the scion section is fruit-bearing age —
  fruit in 1–2 years instead of 5–7
- Learn in a weekend workshop; scionwood exchanges are a network tradition
  (winter scion swaps travel through the surplus routes like everything else)

### 4. The species shortcut list (fast-fruiting perennials for year one)
| Perennial | Time to fruit if planted mature | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Strawberries | 0 (same spring) | runners = free mature stock |
| Bush berries (blue, currant, goose) | 0–1 yr | buy 2–3 yr containers |
| Cane berries (black/raspberry) | 0–1 yr | root divisions |
| Figs, mulberries, persimmon | 1–2 yr | fast, forgiving transplanters |
| Pears, plums, apples (bare-root 3–5yr) | 1–3 yr | plant dormant |
| Asparagus (2-yr crowns) | 1 yr (light) | 3-yr wait eliminated |
| Rhubarb, horseradish, comfrey | 0 (division) | immediate |
| Passionflower, hardy kiwi, grapes | 1–2 yr | mature vines |

### 5. The transplant rules (why transplants fail — avoid these)
1. **Dormant season moves** (winter/early spring) — the shock window is tiny
2. **The root-to-top ratio:** transplanting cuts roots; cut the canopy to
   match (30–50% reduction on big moves) — the tree fruits sooner balanced
3. **Water the HOLE, not just the plant**; mulch 6 inches immediately
4. **First-fruit thinning:** remove most fruit year one; let ONE mature
   branch's fruit set to learn flavor — energy goes to roots first
5. **Plant the guild the same day** — comfrey/chives/garlic around the new
   mature tree from day one (the guild IS the irrigation system)

## The Network Nursery (the compounding machine)

- **Every community's nursery maintains mature-aged stock** — 2–3 year
  trees, fruiting brambles, divided crowns — not just seedlings
- **Surplus mature stock travels the routes:** a new community plants from
  other communities' mature divisions and gets a harvest-bearing food forest
  in years one-two, not five-seven
- **Scionwood + runner + division = the three free mature-stock streams**;
  the seed bank's older sibling is the **division bank** (the ledger tracks
  both)
- The one-person start (one-person-start.md) plugs in: porch propagation
  grows division stock for years before land — arrive with mature stock, not
  seedlings

## Kernel Verdict

- **Year-one fruit is achievable** by planting AGE (mature bare-root trees,
  fruiting containers, divisions, 2-yr crowns, grafted scions) instead of
  seed — the standard seed-first timeline is a choice, not a law
- The techniques are nearly free (division, runners, cuttings, scionwood)
  with purchase as the accelerant (dormant-season sales, culls, network stock)
- The network makes it permanent: **every community is a mature-stock nursery
  for the next** — the division bank travels beside the seed bank, and each
  new founder inherits harvest years, not waiting years
