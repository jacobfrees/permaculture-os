# 00-START-HERE — The One-Person Start (Full-Time Job Edition)
*One person. A full-time job. A growing community. Mostly 15-minute daily
attention. Feasible? Yes — this is the design. Start indoors.*

## Part 1 — The Honest Feasibility Answer

**Feasible: yes. Instant: no.** The design principle: **a permaculture system,
once established, is designed to run on low attention** — that is literally
what permaculture means (permanent + agriculture): design so the system does
the work. A mature food forest needs a few focused hours a week, not daily
labor. The hard part is only the first 1–2 years, and there are tools for
that (below).

The full-time-job founder's real constraint is **attention, not time**, and
the system is designed for it:

- **Outdoor work is batched** into a few weekend blocks per month (digging
  swales, planting trees, building)
- **Daily attention is 15 minutes** — evening checks, small watering,
  harvest-to-dinner
- **Automation does the boring parts** — timers, drip irrigation on
  battery/solar pumps, gravity-fed water = the system waters itself

## Part 2 — Start Indoors: The Apartment/Porch Bootstrap ✅

**You can build 80% of the foundation with zero land.** This is the real
answer: start where you are, on a porch/balcony/windowsill. Everything below
runs while you're at work.

### Stage I — The Kitchen Lab (indoors, 15 min/day)
1. **Microgreens** — the perfect busy-person crop: sow trays Sunday (10 min),
   water twice (5 min), harvest Friday (10 min). Nutrient-dense, 7–14 day
   cycle, $0.50/tray cost → $15–25/tray market value. A shelf = salads for
   you + surplus to sell/gift
2. **Sprouting jars** — lentils, radish, broccoli: 2 min of rinsing daily,
   the cheapest nutrition in agriculture
3. **Windowsill medicinals** — mint, basil, tulsi, aloe, rosemary: start the
   pharmacy immediately, learn propagation from week one
4. **A fermentation station** — sauerkraut/kimchi/kombucha: 10 min a week,
   teaches the preservation skills the community will need, feeds you

### Stage II — The Porch System (balcony/porch scale)
1. **Drip + timer containers** — $40 timer + $30 drip kit = the system
   waters itself while you work; tomatoes, peppers, herbs, greens
2. **5-gallon bucket gardens** — the universal container (drill holes,
   you're done); potatoes, sweet potatoes, ginger, turmeric
3. **The porch propagation bench** — cuttings, divisions, seed-starting:
   this is where the future community's plant stock begins. Every mature
   nursery plant you own = one less thing to buy at the land
4. **Compost bucket + worm bin** — your kitchen scraps become the first
   soil factory; worms are the smallest livestock with the biggest returns

### Stage III — The Land Anchor (weekend batches)
- When land is secured (or even a friend's yard — START THERE), weekend
  batches plant what the porch grew. **The porch nursery means your land
  work is transplanting, not seedling care** — the highest-value use of
  scarce weekends
- Swales and earthworks: batched into 2–3 work parties (the crew is
  recruited from the people already eating your porch surplus)

## Part 3 — The Weekend Batch Schedule (the full-time job founder's calendar)

| Cadence | Task block | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Daily (evening) | water check, harvest-to-dinner, 2 min notes | 15 min |
| Weekly (Sunday) | sow microgreens, propagation, fermentation, seed saving | 2–3 h |
| Monthly (weekend) | land day: planting, earthworks progress, build tasks | 6–8 h |
| Quarterly | adaptation report to the kernel; modality/health check | 4 h |
| Seasonally | work party (recruit from eaters!), bulk buy, preserve days | 1–2 days |

**The first crew grows out of the harvest.** Friends who eat porch tomatoes
become Sunday helpers become work-party members become the first Covenant
signers. The porch surplus IS the recruitment engine — same principle at
every scale.

## Part 4 — The Automation Stack (cheap, low-tech, proven)

1. **Gravity water** — an elevated tank (IBC tote on a stand) + drip lines =
   zero-pump watering, runs for days unattended
2. **Battery timers** — $15 irrigation timers handle everything while you work
3. **Mulch thickly** — 6+ inches cuts watering 70%; mulch is the highest-ROI
   labor in the whole system
4. **Self-sowing guild plants** — calendula, dill, cilantro, comfrey replant
   themselves; choose species that volunteer
5. **Chickens/ducks (when land arrives)** — the automated weeders, pest
   control, and fertilizers; move the pen, they work the bed
6. **Mushroom logs + bins** — near-zero attention protein/medicine (oyster
   mushrooms on straw: 5 min/week, harvest for months)

## Part 5 — The Exponential Design (why it grows on little attention)

1. **Perennials compound** — every tree/bush planted once produces for
   decades with zero replanting; asparagus, rhubarb, herbs, berries all
   "come back on their own"
2. **Self-multiplying stock** — comfrey, mint, garlic, strawberries expand
   their footprint themselves; division doubles stock yearly for free
3. **Seed banks compound** — one seed head saved = 200 seeds next year
4. **People compound fastest** — each recruited member brings hands AND
   skills; the load divides as the system multiplies (this is why the
   Covenant and governance docs exist BEFORE the community arrives)
5. **Knowledge compounds** — every adaptation report you file makes the next
   founder's year one easier; the network is your invisible crew

## Part 6 — The Realistic First-Year Math (one person, full-time job)

- **Money out:** $200–500 (tools, seeds, buckets, timer, drip kit, starts)
- **Time:** ~15 min/day + 2 weekends/month
- **Output by year end:** a running porch nursery (200+ plants), a soil
  factory going, fermented pantry, 10–20 species experience, a Sunday crew
  of 2–5, and the seed/beginnings bank for the land phase
- **By year 2–3 (with land):** swales planted from your own stock, first
  harvests, first Covenant signers, first kernel reports filed

**The honest tradeoff:** with a full-time job, year one outdoors is slower
than a full-time founder's — but the porch phase means you START NOW, and
every element (nursery, soil factory, crew, kernel reports) transfers to the
land intact. The full-time-job founder typically arrives at the land with
MORE preparation than the impatient one — just later.

## Kernel Verdict

- **Feasible: yes** — permaculture is literally the design of systems that
  grow on little attention; the first two years are the only attention-heavy
  part, and the porch/indoor phase absorbs most of it
- **Does not have to be outdoor first** — 80% of the foundation (nursery,
  soil factory, preservation skills, crew, documentation) builds indoors or
  on a porch while you work
- **Microgreens/indoors are the perfect founder crop** — fastest cycle,
  highest value density, teaches every skill the land phase needs
- Start tonight: one tray of microgreens, one jar of sprouts, one mint
  cutting. That is Stage I of the whole civilization stack.
