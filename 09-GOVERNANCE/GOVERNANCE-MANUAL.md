# GOVERNANCE MANUAL
### [COMMUNITY NAME] — v0.1 (forkable template)
*Companion to the Covenant. Read the Covenant first — this manual only explains how we keep its promises.*

---

## 1. First Principles

1. **No separation.** There is no "us above you." No member's voice weighs more — not a founder's, not an elder's, not a group's. Every decision-making structure below exists only to let *everyone* steer together, and any structure that starts separating members from one another is dissolved and redesigned.
2. **Power is exercised, never owned.** Roles are temporary. Knowledge is shared. Every critical system always has at least two people fully trained on it.
3. **Consent over majority.** We don't outvote each other. We decide only what no member reasonably objects to.
4. **The law of the land is followed, fully.** Compliance is our strategy and our duty (see §9).
5. **The Covenant outranks this manual.** Where they conflict, the Covenant wins.

## 2. Membership

- **Joining:** by attending openly, contributing visibly, and signing the Covenant. Any existing member may support but none may veto; the community grows by attraction, not gatekeeping.
- **Status:** every member is identical in standing from day one. No probation tiers, no novice class, no second-class membership.
- **Leaving:** notice given at any time. Departing members keep all personal property and savings and are honored at their going. An exit conversation (see §10) turns every departure into a governance lesson.
- **Boundaries:** members agree only to the Covenant and to the specific work agreements they personally consent to. Nothing else.

## 3. Circles — How Work Organizes Itself

Work is organized into **circles**, not departments of authority. A circle is simply everyone who does a kind of work together: Water, Soil & Food, Shelter, Energy, Sanitation, Education, Medicine, Economy, Liaison (outside relations), and others as needed.

- Each circle **facilitates** rather than leads. Facilitation rotates at a set interval (default: 3 months) with a written handover checklist.
- Circles decide their own internal work by consent of their members.
- Matters affecting the whole community go to the **Whole Circle** (§4).
- Any member may sit in any circle at any time. Circle boundaries are porous on purpose — separation is what we are designed to prevent.

## 4. The Whole Circle — Community Decisions

- **Who:** every member. Attendance is open; no quorum can exclude a member from a decision that affects them.
- **Facilitated by:** a rotating facilitator whose only powers are order-of-business and equalizing airtime. The facilitator has no vote advantage and no agenda.
- **Decision method:** consent. Proposals are amended until no member holds a reasoned objection. One reasoned objection pauses the proposal — it does not kill it; the objection is worked *with*.
- **Devil's Advocate:** a formal rotating office. In every significant decision, one member is *assigned* to argue against it. Refusing the role is fine; skipping the role is not.
- **Log:** every decision, objection, and resolution is recorded in the community ledger, version-controlled, and public (§7).

## 5. Rotation & Replacement (the anti-irreplaceability engine)

- Every role rotates on a schedule and hands over with a written checklist.
- **Two-deep rule:** every critical function (water, funds, medicine, legal filings) must have at least two people fully capable of running it at all times. A function with one capable person is treated as an emergency and fixed before any expansion work.
- Knowledge transfer is a standing duty: each-one-teach-one. Hoarding knowledge is a Covenant violation of "what one knows, all may learn."

## 6. Economy

- **Ledger of reciprocity, not wages:** contributions of labor, goods, and teaching are recorded transparently. The ledger informs gratitude and coordination — it does not buy power. No quantity of contribution grants decision weight beyond one equal voice.
- Personal property, outside income, and savings remain private to each member. Only **community** finances are public.
- Community funds: dual authorization for any spending, public record of every transaction, no exceptions for anyone.

## 7. Transparency

- All community finances, decisions, meeting logs, and the Covenant and this manual are public — to members, to neighbors, to officials, to the world.
- The anonymous feedback channel (over the community mesh) is always open, monitored by a rotating steward, and reported to the Whole Circle without identifying authors.
- There is no private teaching of any kind. All education materials live in one public library.

## 8. Dissent & Conflict

- Objections are invited in every meeting and accepted in writing at any time.
- Conflict process: direct conversation → mediated conversation (a neutral member) → circle consultation → Whole Circle consent process. No step may pressure a person to withdraw an objection.
- **Banned patterns, named explicitly:** slogans as answers; "the group knows best"; "you'll understand when you're ready"; punishing questioners; shunning in any form. Any member may call a "red flag check" at any time — the Whole Circle immediately examines itself against §1 and the Covenant.

## 9. Law & Government Relations

- We follow all applicable laws: permits, codes, taxes, records, water rights, employment, and health regulation. The community's Law-Navigation work exists to make compliance achievable, never to evade it.
- **Disqualifying behavior:** tax protest schemes, false identities, "sovereign citizen" claims, concealment from officials. These endanger every member and violate the Covenant's integrity commitment.
- **Civic participation is a role:** voting, jury service, county meetings, and local volunteering are supported with community labor where needed.
- **Officials are neighbors:** sheriffs, commissioners, inspectors, and health officers are briefed on the community, toured through it, given our public ledger on request, and invited to harvest days.
- Community services (health, education, emergency prep) **integrate with** public services; they never replace or obstruct them.

## 10. The Annual Self-Audit

Each year, the community examines itself against this list, and a **sister community** (a peer community chosen for outside eyes) sends a visiting member to review and file an honest report:

1. Is any one person irreplaceable anywhere in our systems?
2. Can every member leave today with zero friction — and would they feel free to?
3. Was every objection this year heard, logged, and worked with?
4. Are finances accurate, dual-authorized, and public?
5. Does every member have active outside relationships, and outside work or income if they want it?
6. Is any knowledge or teaching unavailable to any member?
7. Is dissent comfortable here right now — cheaper to voice than to withhold?
8. Has any structure begun separating members from one another?

**Rule:** any "no" or failing answer pauses all expansion until it is fixed. Growth never outranks integrity.

## 11. Amendment & Forking

- This manual may be amended by full consent; the Covenant by unanimous consent.
- Every version is kept in public version history with its reasoning — our governance improves the way open-source software does.
- **Any community anywhere may fork this entire document.** Adapt it to your land, your law, your people — and share your patches back, so that every community's hard-won lesson becomes every community's starting point.

---

*Questions about this manual are always in order. The most important question any member can ask — "why do we do it this way?" — has a standing answer: because we are all equal here, and if a way of doing things ever makes us unequal, we change the way of doing things.*
