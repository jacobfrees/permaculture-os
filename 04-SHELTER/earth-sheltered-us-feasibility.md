# 04-SHELTER — Hobbit / Earth-Sheltered Homes: US-Wide Feasibility
*Short answer: feasible in every US state, as long as the design is engineered,
stamped, and follows code. Regional climate adaptations below.*

## Why Earth-Sheltered Works Everywhere (in principle)

An earth-sheltered home is not exotic technology — it is a reinforced structure
(concrete, ICF, earthbag, or insulated mass walls) with waterproofing, insulation,
and soil against/over it. Every US state building code accommodates this via
engineered-design provisions. The dirt berm and sod roof are typically treated
as landscaping, not structure — which makes permitting *easier* than domes or
tire walls in most jurisdictions.

## Regional Adaptation Table

| Region | Climate challenge | Required adaptation | Feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Northeast** (NY, VT, ME...) | Frost depth 4–6 ft, snow load | Frost-protected shallow footings or footings below frost line; roof designed for snow load; strong vapor barrier | ★★★★★ — earth-sheltering was practically invented here (Malcolm Wells, NJ/MA) |
| **Upper Midwest** (MN, WI, MI) | Extreme cold, deep frost | Extra roof insulation (R-40+), south glazing maximized, entry airlocks | ★★★★★ — underground homes common in MN/IL since the 1970s |
| **Southeast / Humid** (NC, GA, TN) | 45–55 in/yr rain, humidity/mold, hurricanes, termites | Insulated thermal mass (NOT bare mass), full waterproof membrane, dehumidification strategy, termite shields, big roof overhangs; see nc-climate-building-methods.md | ★★★★☆ — excellent with moisture discipline |
| **Gulf Coast** (LA, FL, TX coast) | Hurricanes, flood, water table, termites | Must build ABOVE flood elevation — bermed/partially buried only on high ground; elevated structures in flood zones; mold discipline | ★★★☆☆ — flood zones are the limit; inland spots great |
| **Great Plains** (KS, NE, OK) | Tornadoes, wind, hail, temperature swings | Earth-sheltering is the BEST answer here — berms on 3 sides + sod roof = near-tornado-proof; storm-safe by design | ★★★★★ — the bermed house is arguably the original Plains house (soddy) |
| **Southwest Desert** (NM, AZ, NV) | Heat, aridity, monsoon flash rain | Earthships work nearly as-designed (this is their home); mass walls shine; watch summer ventilation | ★★★★★ — easiest place, fewest restrictions (rural counties) |
| **Mountain West** (CO, MT, WY) | Snow load, freeze-thaw, altitude sun | Snow-load roof engineering, foundation below frost, winter entry planning | ★★★★☆ |
| **Pacific NW** (WA, OR) | Rain, mold, seismic | Waterproofing + drainage like the Southeast, plus seismic engineering west of the Cascades | ★★★★☆ |
| **California** | Seismic, fire, strict energy code (Title 24) | Seismic engineering standard; berms help fire resilience; code strictness = more engineering cost | ★★★☆☆ — doable, costliest stamps |

## The Universal Rules (any state)

1. **Engineer + stamp first** — every state accepts engineered designs; the
   engineering cost ($2–5k) is the price of admission everywhere
2. **Waterproofing is sacred** — membrane + drainage plane + footing drains.
   90% of earth-shelter failures are water, not structure
3. **Insulate the mass** — bare mass against soil loses to ground temperature;
   R-10+ between living space and earth (the universal fix for the earthship
   thermal mistake)
4. **Perc test before land purchase** — septic gates everything (see Law-Navigation)
5. **County call before land purchase** — ask the hypothetical; rank counties
   before buying
6. **South glazing + overhang** (northern hemisphere) — winter gain, summer shade
7. **File your build back to the Legal Atlas** — county answers, permit timeline,
   costs — so the next founder starts ahead

## The Growing-Community Sequence

- Home 1: earth-bermed rectilinear (easiest permit, fastest to occupancy)
- Home 2+: domes, aircrete, hybrid — now that the county has seen your work
- Shared buildings under the agricultural exemption where applicable
- Each build files an adaptation report: region, county, what officials asked

*Verdict: the hobbit home is one of the few shelter forms feasible in ALL 50
states with only regional tuning — because it uses the two materials every
state has: dirt and code.*
