# 04-SHELTER — Site Shaping
*The shelter pad, drainage, and thermal strategy — before any wall goes up.*

## Order of Operations (never skip ahead)

1. **Water design first** (see 01-WATER) — the shelter site must be OUT of drainage paths
2. **Contour map** the building zone at 1m intervals (A-frame or laser level)
3. **Pad selection:** slight high ground, south-facing slope (N. hemisphere) of 5–15% ideal
4. **Cut-and-fill pad** — dig the high side, fill the low side, compact in 15cm lifts
5. **Drainage:** swale ABOVE the site (intercepts upslope water), French drain BELOW, roof catchment planned from day one
6. **Earth tubes** — bury pipe runs 2–3m deep on the north side while the pad is open (free heating/cooling forever; impossible to retrofit cheaply)

## Earthworks for the Pad

| Task | By hand | Mini excavator |
|---|---|---|
| 60m² flat pad on slope | 2–4 weeks | 1–2 days |
| Earth-tube trenching (30m) | 3–5 days | 2–4 hours |
| Drainage swale + berm | 3–5 days | 1 day |

See `01-WATER/earthmoving-options.md` for the full machine ladder, capacities, and rental costs.

## Passive Design Rules (build them into the SHAPING, not the building)

- **Orientation:** long axis east–west; main glazing within 15° of true south (northern hemisphere)
- **Earth sheltering:** berm the north and west walls with pad-excavation dirt —
  cut-and-fill again; the dirt never leaves the site
- **Earth tubes:** ~15°C ground temperature year-round = pre-heated winter air, pre-cooled summer air
- **Drain the site, then the wall:** water management failures cause 90% of earth-shelter failures

## Compaction

- Stomp + sprinkle in lifts for berms; plate compactor ($40–60/day rental) for pads
- Earthbag walls: each course barbed-wire + tamped = structure and bond in one
