# 04-SHELTER — RV-in-Bay Barndominium: Storm Garage + Home Base
*The mobile-home-inside-the-shelter concept: an oversized bay built around an
RV/travel trailer — hurricane/hail/tornado protection, instant furnished
quarters, and a home base that rolls or stays.*

## The Concept

A barndominium (steel-frame or pole-barn shell with living quarters) with one
bay sized to fully enclose an RV or travel trailer:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│  living loft / kitchen / bath       │
├───────────────┬─────────────────────┤
│   RV BAY      │   workshop /        │
│  (12–16 ft    │   storage /         │
│   door ht)    │   garden processing │
│  [RV inside]  │                     │
└───▲───────────┴─────────────────────┘
    14 ft insulated roll door
```

## Why This Is a Genuinely Smart Permaculture Move

1. **Instant livable shelter** — move the RV in on day 1 and you have a bed,
   kitchen, and bathroom while the main build happens. The bootstrap dwelling
   problem (live WHERE while building?) is solved
2. **Storm protection** — the RV is shielded from hail, wind, falling limbs;
   in hurricane/tornado country the steel bay is a cheap "personal storm
   garage." Double protection: metal shell + earth berm on the RV-bay side
3. **The RV becomes infrastructure** — water tank = emergency storage, propane
   = backup heat/cooking, battery/solar = backup power, and the whole rig can
   still drive to town or evacuate with everything you own already packed
4. **Legal simplicity** — a bay with a vehicle in it is just a garage; the RV
   is "stored property," which no code office blinks at
5. **Reusable pattern** — the bay later becomes workshop, food-processing
   kitchen, or visitor quarters; nothing is wasted

## Feasibility & Code Notes

- **Barndominiums are mainstream** — post-frame/steel building with living
  quarters is a standard, code-friendly path in most rural US counties,
  including NC. Permits as a single-family dwelling with an accessory garage
- **RV as temporary dwelling:** most jurisdictions allow short-term occupancy
  of an RV on your own land (commonly 14–180 days, varies by county) — check
  your county's RV occupancy rule; sleeping in the RV inside your own building
  during construction is generally tolerated but CONFIRM, don't assume
- **Permanent living in the RV** is usually NOT allowed as a substitute for a
  dwelling — the barndominium's living quarters are the legal home; the RV is
  the guest room / backup
- **Bay dimensions:** Class C + clearance = 12 ft wide × 30–35 ft deep ×
  13–14 ft door. Class A diesel needs 14–16 ft door height
- **Add while building (cheap now, costly later):** 50-amp RV outlet, water
  spigot/hydrant, floor drain, RV-length concrete pad with a slight grade,
  graywater bucket area
- **Fire/separation:** some jurisdictions want a fire-rated wall between
  dwelling and vehicle bay — cheap to design in from the start
- **Budget:** shell + RV bay runs roughly $40–80/sq ft finished-living /
  $25–40/sq ft bay-only; often the CHEAPEST legal, permitted, livable structure
  on this whole shelf

## Kernel Verdict

For a founding member arriving on raw land: **RV + RV-bay barndominium is the
fastest legal path to "sheltered, dry, powered, cooking" — often within a
month** — while the earth-bermed or dome home rises nearby. The RV bay then
serves the community forever as storm garage, guest room, or workshop.
