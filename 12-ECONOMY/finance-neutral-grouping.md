# 12-ECONOMY — Low-Income Sustainability & Finance-Neutral Grouping
*How to live well on minimum income, and how people group up closely without
financially tying or restricting each other — everyone grows together.*

## Part 1 — The Dollar Multiplier (why community income goes further)

A dollar spent inside a permaculture community buys what a city dollar cannot,
because the expensive things in life are already solved by the land and the group:

| Life cost (city) | Community version | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| Rent/mortgage ($1,200–2,000/mo) | Small legal dwelling + shared land trust; build equity in the trust, not a landlord | ~10x |
| Groceries ($600–900/mo) | Food forest + shared bulk buys + preservation | ~5x |
| Electric ($150–300/mo) | Solar + thermal mass + earth tubes | ~5x |
| Water/sewer ($80–150/mo) | Rain catchment + compost sanitation | ~10x |
| Transport ($500–900/mo) | Shared vehicles + remote work + on-site living | ~4x |
| Childcare ($800–1,600/mo) | Each-one-teach-one education on site | ~8x |
| Fitness/wellness ($100–300/mo) | Physical work + garden food + community care | ~10x |

**The pattern: the highest-leverage dollar is the one that builds infrastructure
that eliminates a recurring bill.** $6,000 of rainwater cistern deletes $100/mo
forever. A $15k tiny dwelling deletes rent forever. The community's collective
"dividend" is measured in deleted bills.

### The minimum-income sustainability stack
1. **Delete bills before earning more** — shelter, water, food, energy are 70%
   of a low income; the land pays those
2. **Shared bulk economics** — one 50-lb bag of rice per family is expensive;
   ten families ordering direct from farms cuts food cost 60%+
3. **Skill trading replaces service spending** — haircuts, repairs, childcare,
   teaching flow through reciprocity, not cash
4. **Seasonal surplus income** — farm income, builders, preserved-goods sales,
   teaching workshops — timed around the food calendar, not against it
5. **The barndo/RV ladder** — live for $300/mo equivalents while building
   equity in real things

## Part 2 — Finance-Neutral Grouping (the core design)

**The problem being solved:** conventional "intentional community" finance
fails because it ties people: communal income pooling, shared debt, buy-in
equity — every model either traps someone who wants to leave, or excludes
someone who can't buy in. The kernel design: **share costs, never share wealth.**

### The Principle: Costs Shared, Wealth Separate

```
SHARED (the commons)          PERSONAL (untouchable)
─────────────────────         ─────────────────────
land (held by land trust)     income & wages
shared structures             savings & investments
utilities & systems           personal property
bulk food orders              debts & obligations
vehicles & tools              business ventures
                              retirement accounts
                              the right to leave with it all
```

- **No one pools income.** Every member keeps 100% of what they earn, however
  they earn it — job, freelance, benefits, business, inheritance
- **No one carries another's debt.** The community never co-signs, never
  guarantees, never borrows against a member's future
- **No buy-in requirement.** Membership is earned by participation and the
  Covenant — never by check. (A land trust solves how land gets bought without
  members buying in — see below)
- **No one can be financially trapped.** Because personal wealth is untouched
  and no equity stake is required to belong, leaving costs a person nothing —
  the sacred exit rule stays intact even after 20 years

### How land gets held without tying anyone

**The land trust with leaseholds (the standard kernel model):**
- A nonprofit land trust (community-controlled, one-member-one-vote) owns the
  land permanently — removes it from the speculative market forever
- Members hold **99-year renewable leaseholds** on their home sites — real,
  secure, inheritable, sellable-back-at-cost — for a modest monthly ground
  lease fee that funds the commons
- **The result:** a member with $2,000 to their name and a member with
  $200,000 can live as equals. Neither buys in. Both build equity in their
  dwelling (personal property). The land belongs to everyone by belonging to
  no individual
- If a member leaves: they sell or gift their dwelling to an incoming member
  at transparent cost; the lease returns to the trust. No one profits off
  scarcity; no one loses their savings

### Shared costs that scale with each person

The commons fund covers land lease payments, shared insurance, shared tools,
bulk purchases, and communal infrastructure. Contributions:
- **A base share, identical for everyone** — the commons never charges
  proportional-to-income, because that creates surveillance of personal wealth
- **Work-in-kind accepted at published rates** — anyone short on cash pays in
  labor, teaching, produce, or skills. Money is one currency of contribution,
  never the only one
- **No minimum income to join** — a broke-but-present member is a full member;
  the reciprocity ledger (Covenant §6) tracks contribution, not cash

### Growing incomes TOGETHER without tying them

This is the subtle part — the community actively multiplies each person's
earning power while keeping all earnings separate:

1. **The skill guild effect** — members train each other (each-one-teach-one)
   in marketable skills: construction, plumbing, food production, teaching,
   coding. The community is a free trade school; each graduate earns more
   *individually*
2. **Shared reputation** — client work flows to members through the
   community's public track record ("the crew that built X") — but the
   invoices go to the individual, never the group
3. **Cooperative ventures, opt-in only** — a market garden, a construction
   crew, a preserve business: formed as separate co-ops that members MAY join
   with transparent, written terms — and may leave annually. Never a mandatory
   venture, never a shared liability
4. **The emergency floor, by reciprocity** — members in a bad month (job loss,
   illness) get the commons' help: work available, food available, rent
   deferred via ledger. It's mutual aid between individuals, organized —
   not an insurance pool with rules and exclusions
5. **Capital access without lending** — members with surplus may LEND to
   members with opportunity — but always as personal, documented, no-community-
   liability loans. The community's role is the transparent ledger, not the
   middleman

### What this design refuses (and why)

| Refused | Because it |
|---|---|
| Income pooling | creates surveillance, resentment, and trapped members |
| Communal debt | makes one person's dream everyone's obligation |
| Buy-in equity | excludes the poor; cages the leaver |
| Profit-sharing on land | turns members into investors, community into LLC |
| Proportional dues | requires auditing private wealth |

Each refusal protects the two sacred properties: **exit is always free, and
one person's money never buys standing.**

## Kernel Verdict

- **Minimum income works here** because the land deletes the four biggest bills
  (shelter, food, water, energy) and the group deletes the rest (childcare,
  transport, skills)
- **People group up closely without financial ties** by sharing *costs* through
  the land trust and commons fund while keeping *wealth* individual — nobody's
  income, debt, or savings touches anyone else's
- **Everyone grows together** through the skill guild, shared reputation, and
  opt-in co-ops — the community multiplies each person's earning power and
  collects zero percent of the result
- The reciprocity ledger records what people contribute so the generous are
  seen; money is never what it records first

and GOVERNANCE-MANUAL §6 (the ledger never buys power).*
