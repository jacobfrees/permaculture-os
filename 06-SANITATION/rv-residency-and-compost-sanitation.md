# 06-SANITATION + 10-LAW-NAVIGATION — Temporary RV Residency & Compost Sanitation
*How to live on your land full-time legally, and how to make the county case
for composting sanitation. All paths are legitimate ones — the kernel's rule
is compliance as strategy.*

## Part 1 — What Actually Limits Full-Time RV Living

For most rural US counties, full-time occupancy on your own land is gated by
THREE things, not one:

| Gate | What it is | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Sewage disposal** | An approved method for human waste — usually a permitted septic system | **The hard gate** — yes, usually the deciding factor |
| **2. Water source** | Permitted well, approved cistern/rainwater system, or municipal hook-up | Moderate — rainwater/cistern rules vary by state |
| **3. Dwelling definition** | The county's rules on what counts as a legal dwelling, and RV-occupancy day limits | Varies wildly by county |

So: **septic is the biggest gate but not the only one.** A full-time resident
needs all three. The good news: each has legitimate doors.

## Part 2 — Legitimate Paths to Full-Time Residency (the doors, in order)

1. **The dwelling-under-construction exemption.** Most counties allow RV
   occupancy DURING active construction of a permitted dwelling (commonly
   6–24 months, some counties issue renewable temporary-use permits). Get the
   barndominium/main build permitted FIRST, then ask for the construction-
   occupancy window in writing. This converts "camping illegally" into
   "builder's quarters," which most counties issue routinely
2. **The temporary use permit (TUP).** Many counties have a formal permit for
   exactly this situation — renewable, condition-based (usually: approved
   sewage, approved water, active project). Ask the planning department for
   their TUP application by name; it's a normal document, not a favor
3. **Agricultural worker/farm occupancy provisions.** Farming states commonly
   allow temporary dwellings for agricultural use on farmland. A permaculture
   operation with a farm plan fits several states' ag-occupancy provisions
4. **Build the tiny legal dwelling FIRST.** A minimal code-compliant home
   (even 400 sq ft, many counties' minimum or ADU-size) with approved septic
   makes full-time residency simply legal. The RV then lives in its bay as the
   guest/backup quarters (see rv-bay-barndominium.md). **Sequencing trick:
   septic + smallest legal dwelling = legal full-time residency in year one**
5. **Change the rule (the slow door).** Counties amend ordinances. A
   well-organized, transparent group proposing a composting-toilet + rainwater
   ordinance pilot — with engineers, data, and county officials at the table —
   has changed rules in multiple states. This is the slow, permanent door

**NOT doors (named because the community will encounter them):** calling the
RV a "guest," address games, skipping the septic permit, "no one will notice."
These end in removal orders, fines, and a poisoned relationship with the one
office that approves everything else.

## Part 3 — Making the Case for Compost Sanitation Systems

### The framing that wins: reduce, not replace
Counties fear novel sewage. The winning frame is: **"a certified composting
toilet REDUCES the septic load — so the required drainfield gets smaller and
cheaper, and the groundwater gets safer."** That is a public-health argument,
which is the only language a health department speaks.

### The specifics that get approvals

1. **NSF/ANSI 41 certification is the master key.** Residential composting
   toilets certified to NSF/ANSI 41 (e.g., Clivus Multrum, Phoenix, Sun-Mar
   and similar) are accepted by a growing list of states either outright or
   through a state-review process. DIY buckets are not certifiable — design
   community buildings around certified units, and treat DIY systems as the
   off-grid backup, not the permit path
2. **The hybrid design (most approvable):**
   - NSF-certified composting toilets for BLACKwater (or most of it)
   - A conventional (or reduced-size) septic system for greywater + overflow
   - The composting system shrinks the required drainfield — many health
     departments will approve a SMALLER septic design when the toilet load is
     composted, because flow calculations drop
3. **NC specifics:** NC allows alternative/innovative onsite systems through
   state rules (15A NCAC 18A), and composting toilets are addressed in state
   onsite wastewater provisions — typically requiring the approved septic
   field for greywater/liquid waste anyway. The pitch to an NC health
   department is exactly the hybrid above: "smaller drainfield, certified
   units, engineered submittal"
4. **Bring the engineer.** A PE or REHS-signed submittal converts "hippie
   idea" into "engineered system." Every successful compost-sanitation
   approval had professional cover
5. **Bring the data.** EPA's wastewater documentation recognizes composting
   toilets; NSF listings; state approvals in MA, WA, OR, CO, NM and others.
   The case writes itself: *certified units are legal in X states; here is the
   engineering; here is the smaller drainfield; here is our maintenance plan*
6. **Offer monitoring.** The approver's real fear is "no one checks it."
   Volunteer: annual inspection reports, a maintenance log posted in the
   public ledger (the community is transparent by charter), and a named
   certified operator (composting systems typically want a responsible party)

### The community-scale argument (for later, not first)
Once one approved system runs cleanly for 12 months with logged results, the
community has local precedent. Precedent + the Legal Atlas entry + a county
that knows you = the next approval gets easier. Sequence: **one certified
system → 12 clean months → propose the community-scale design.**

## Kernel Verdict

- Full-time RV living is limited by **sewage + water + dwelling definition** —
  septic is the big one, but the **construction-occupancy window + smallest
  legal dwelling** sequence makes full-time residency achievable in year one
- Compost sanitation is **approvable now** through the certified-unit hybrid
  design; the argument is "smaller drainfield, safer groundwater," delivered
  with an engineer's signature
- Every county interaction gets filed back to the Legal Atlas — each approval
  anywhere teaches every other community the path

*Verify current state/county rules before relying on any door here — codes
change, and this document is a map, not a permit.*
