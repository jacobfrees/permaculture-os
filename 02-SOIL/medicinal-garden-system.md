# 02-SOIL / 07-MEDICINE — The Medicinal Garden System
*Every community grows its own medicine chest. The garden is the pharmacy,
the school, the conservation project, and the economy all at once.*

## Part 1 — How Efficient/Extensive Can a Medicinal Garden Be?

**Short answer: extremely.** A well-designed half-acre medicinal garden can:

- **Supply the full T1 (home & garden pharmacy) needs of 50–150 people** —
  teas, tinctures, salves, syrups, poultices for everyday complaints
  (colds, wounds, sleep, digestion, pain, skin, stress)
- **Produce 60–120 distinct species** in the same footprint (medicinals are
  the natural fit for the 7-layer food forest — most are already there)
- **Yield surpluses worth real money**: dried herbs ($8–30/lb), tinctures,
  salves, seedlings, and seed are among the highest value-per-acre crops in
  agriculture — a medicinal garden can out-earn vegetables per square foot
- **Conserve species**: botanical gardens and permaculture sites are among
  the only places at-risk medicinal plants still survive

### Realistic production numbers (temperate half-acre, mature)
| Output | Quantity/yr | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Dried herb (tea/infusion) | 200–400 lbs | 50+ people's daily tea + tincture base |
| Tincture stock (fresh herb) | 300–500 lbs processed | ~50–100 gallons finished tincture |
| Salve/oil herbs (calendula, comfrey, plantain) | 100–200 lbs | skin care for whole community + gifts |
| Culinary-medicinal (garlic, sage, thyme, rosemary, oregano) | overlaps kitchen | double-purpose by design |
| Nursery stock (divisions, cuttings, seedlings) | 2,000–5,000 plants | community expansion + traveler sales |
| Seed | 20–50 varieties | the network's most valuable export |

**The multipliers that make it extensive rather than large:**
1. **Layer stacking** — medicinal trees (willow, linden, elderberry) over
   shrubs (elder, rosehip) over herbs (echinacea, calendula) over ground
   covers (self-heal, chickweed) over roots (ginger-family, ashwagandha in
   season) over vines — 6–8 production layers on one footprint
2. **Succession + preservation** — harvest is weekly; drying, tincturing,
   and infusing turn a May flush into year-round medicine
3. **The guild trick** — most food-forest guild plants ARE medicinal
   (comfrey, yarrow, dandelion, nettle, chicory); the medicinal garden is
   30–50% already built inside the food forest. The dedicated beds add the
   demanding/rare species

## Part 2 — The Medicinal Garden System Docs (what every community builds)

### A. The Native Medicine Section (conservation core)
- **Every bioregion has a native pharmacopoeia.** The community's first
  job: learn and grow the native medicinal plants of THEIR land before
  importing exotics. NC examples: goldenseal, ginseng, black cohosh,
  echinacea, passionflower, spicebush, sassafras, Jerusalem artichoke,
  wild cherry, tulip poplar
- **Native = adapted, resilient, and conservation-positive.** Growing
  natives relieves wild populations from harvest pressure — the garden is
  the *conservation ark*, and many natives (ginseng, goldenseal) are
  endangered IN THE WILD, making community cultivation genuinely protective
- **Native plant expansion rule:** the community maps its land's existing
  medicinal natives, wild-tends (not wild-harvests) them, propagates them
  in the nursery, and restores them to appropriate microclimates on site —
  the garden expands INTO the ecosystem, not over it

### B. The Cultivated Medicine Section (workhorse beds)
- Raised beds + guild plantings for the high-demand herbs: calendula,
  echinacea, chamomile, lemon balm, mint family, lavender, comfrey,
  yarrow, elderberry, rosemary/thyme/sage, garlic, ginger (in season),
  tulsi, ashwagandha (annual in temperate)
- Organized by **use-class** (wound, respiratory, digestive, nervous,
  immune) so education and harvest both work

### C. The Processing Chain (garden → medicine)
1. **Harvest** (the right week, the right part, the right time of day —
   each species has its rule; the practice files carry it)
2. **Drying** — solar dehydrator + drying shed (kernel designs, 05-ENERGY)
3. **Extraction** — tinctures (alcohol/glycerine/vinegar), oils, salves,
   syrups, honeys, steams, poultices
4. **Storage** — amber glass, labeled, dated; the medicine chest is
   inventoried like the seed bank
5. **The pharmacopoeia ledger** — every batch logged: species, date,
   process, who made it, test results (T1 training includes safety dosing)

### D. The Education Layer (the garden IS the classroom)
- Each-one-teach-one: every member learns 20 core plants deeply before
  branching — identification, harvest, preparation, safety, contraindications
- **Seasonal medicine walks** — the classic teaching format; members and
  travelers learn the land's pharmacy in place, from a person, not a screen
- The modality commons (see 07-MEDICINE/modality-commons.md) rotates
  herbalists through residencies; each residency adds its knowledge to the
  kernel documentation

### E. The Conservation Commitment
- Never wild-harvest endangered species; grow or go without
- Seed saving of every medicinal grown — contributed to the network seed
  bank (the most valuable export any community produces)
- Native propagation targets: each community commits to *increasing* its
  bioregion's native medicinal populations on-site, year over year

## Part 3 — Scaling Table (what footprint for what population)

| Population | Garden footprint | Layers in production |
|---|---|---|
| 1–15 people (founding) | 1/10–1/4 acre | kitchen-medicinals + native section begun |
| 15–50 | 1/4–1/2 acre | full 7-layer + processing chain |
| 50–150 | 1/2–1 acre | + nursery, + surplus economy |
| Network node (regional) | 1–3 acres | + seed bank, + teaching center, + conservation restorations |

*These footprints assume the food-forest guild overlap (the 30–50% already
there); they are modest by farming standards — a family farm is 100+ acres,
the medicine chest of 150 people fits inside a suburban block and a half.*

## Part 4 — The Economic Truth

- Medicinal herb products are among the highest value-per-acre crops
  (tinctures, salves, teas, seedlings, seed)
- The seed bank + native propagations are the network's compounding asset —
  every community that starts gets easier, because the seed of every prior
  community flows forward
- Surplus travels the surplus↔need routing protocol: a community rich in
  elderberry sends to a community short; the reciprocity ledger records it
- Where regulations allow, tinctures/salves/teas as cottage-industry
  products fund the commons — check the Legal Atlas for each state's
  cottage-food/herbal-product rules before selling (compliance, always)

## Kernel Verdict

- A half-acre medicinal garden, mature, supplies the everyday-medicine needs
  of 50–150 people and out-earns most crops per square foot
- **Native plant expansion is the conservation arm**: the community restores
  its bioregion's medicinal natives, protects wild populations, and every
  year the land itself gets more medicinally alive, not less
- The garden is simultaneously the pharmacy, the classroom, the seed bank,
  the conservation project, and one of the strongest economies in the network
- Every community plants one in its first season — the seed bank makes sure
  every future community starts with the seed of every prior one
